Write a $page_count-page picture book for this child.

LANGUAGE
$language_rules

LENGTH AND STYLE FOR AGE $age
$age_rules

STRUCTURE
- Page 1 introduces the hero, their world, and one of their favourite things.
- The middle pages build a small, gentle problem connected to the lesson: "$lesson".
- The last page resolves the problem and the hero has learned the lesson.
- Use the hero's name on page 1 and on most other pages.

FIELDS (character and scene fields are ALWAYS in English, even for Hindi stories)
- title: a short, catchy title in the story language.
- hero_name: the child's name exactly as you wrote it in the page text.
- character.description: ENGLISH. One fixed visual description of the hero for an
  illustrator, reused on every page: age, gender if obvious from the brief, hair,
  skin, face details, glasses etc. Base it on the brief's appearance. Do not include the
  name. About 20 words.
- character.outfit: ENGLISH. One specific outfit with colours, worn on every page.
- character.companion: ENGLISH. A recurring animal, or a soft toy animal, drawn from
  the favourites, with colour and size. Never a vehicle or object. null if none fits.
- pages[].number: 1 to $page_count, in order.
- pages[].text: the words printed on the page, in the story language.
- pages[].scene: ENGLISH. What the illustrator should draw: setting, action, and
  the hero's pose and expression. Call the hero "the child", never by name. Do not describe the hero's looks or outfit here
  (they come from character). If there is a companion, it appears in EVERY scene;
  refer to it the same way each time (e.g. "the small grey baby elephant").
  Never ask for written words, signs, or letters. Even worried or night-time moments
  are cosy and softly lit; only the hero, the companion, and named friends or family
  appear (no strangers or figures in the background).
- pages[].mood: one of happy, curious, worried, brave, calm, proud.
